quote: We few, we happy few, we band of brothers.
author: William Shakespeare, Henry V — the St. Crispin's Day speech
source: https://www.folger.edu/explore/shakespeares-works/henry-v/read/4/3/
encountered: sometime in 2011
commentary: |-
  I've come across this quote a few times, in a few places. Most prominently for me, it was in the HBO series Band of Brothers. This was an incredibly formative show. What I admired about the show, even as a child, was how these men could deal with such miserable conditions, whilst still working towards their goal. It is stoicism in a way.

  Additionally, Admiral Nelson constantly called his group of confidants his Band of Brothers — borrowing this very line. Nelson did not hold himself above his men. He kept them in the fold, equal to himself. This is the strongest way, I believe, to be.

  I think it is a sad consequence of modernity that this environment does not arise very often. The many options afforded to people mean they are kept apart. Perhaps you need inescapable circumstance to forge something like this.
